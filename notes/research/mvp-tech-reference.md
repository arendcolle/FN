# What to build the FN MVP prototype on

2026-10-29 · reference for staff and agents working on FN

**TL;DR.** Build one Python app on a laptop: FastAPI pages, one SQLite file, DBOS for durable searches, and Claude Haiku 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 through Pydantic AI. Web search APIs only find candidate URLs. FN fetches every page itself and keeps its own quote and date, which is the only way to meet F5. Six lanes produce candidates: topic web search, precedent pages, OpenAlex, FRIS, SEC EDGAR and seed lists scraped from public pages. One open choice is left, the web search backend, and a gold-set benchmark of about $850 between Parallel, Exa, Parallel FindAll and Perplexity decides it. Expect reliable results on academic and Flemish-research asks first. Business, creative and low-footprint asks rest on precedent pages and are the main risk to the precision target.

This file is the reference. The one-page decision report is a separate Claude Doc. `findings.json` next to this file holds all 322 rated findings with facts checked, probe results and URLs.

### Ties are evidence, not a label (2026-11-02)

FN does not store or compute a tie type. A person's ties are affiliation claims with source, quote and date, the ranker scores those claims directly, and an institution's region is an organization fact from ROR. Any badge or filter is computed from the claims when it is shown. This replaces the "tie typer" and the tie label wherever this file mentions them; `notes/mvp0/architecture.md` has the details.

## What the evidence says

These six facts drive the design. Each comes from a live probe or a vendor page.

1. **No discovery API gives a dated, quotable claim.** Parallel's Basis has no date field and its excerpts can be null. Exa grounding has no documented quote or date layout. Tavily, Linkup, Valyu and You.com sources carry no date. FN has to fetch the page and take the quote and date itself.
2. **Storage terms block keeping vendor output.** Exa ToS 4.2(a) bars copying anything "obtained from or through" the service without written permission, URLs included. Parallel Customer Terms 2(c)(vi)(B), 2(c)(xi) and 4(b) limit databases built from output and reuse across users. Perplexity's Search Addendum 2.3 bars personal data in queries. Gemini grounding bars caching. Keeping only URLs and our own fetched text is the smallest ask under all of them. These terms no longer gate the personal prototype (see Decisions), and they apply again if FN becomes a product.
3. **Raw recall on the gold set measures lookup of past events, not discovery.** Exa found 44 of 74 hard-group picks from the question as typed, but 33 of those sat on the event's own program, post or page. With those pages, LinkedIn and Instagram removed, Exa found 6 and Parallel's keyless default mode found 5, with one overlap.
4. **Structured lanes find the academic and Flemish picks.** OpenAlex found GS-02 3 of 3 and GS-10 4 of 4, each with dated Belgian affiliations. FRIS matched 11 of 13 Flanders-based picks by name and returned 58 projects and 69 people for "6G". SEC EDGAR found De Cock, Burggraeve, Sweldens and Van Hentenryck and no academics.
5. **ORCID, Wikidata and Semantic Scholar alone miss most picks.** ORCID found 0 of 3 GS-02 picks. A Wikidata SPARQL tie query found 0 of 5 MIT picks for GS-10. Wikidata still settles 7 of 22 ties as one step in a lookup order.
6. **Free tiers run out.** OpenAlex has needed a key since February 2026, with $0.10 a day keyless and $1 a day on a free key, and the probe hit HTTP 429 with a wait of about 7,800 s. Semantic Scholar and the GitHub API returned 429 or 403 without a key.

## Design

One search runs as one DBOS workflow. Every outside call is a durable step, so a search survives a restart and can run overnight. Providers return URLs. The Page reader fetches each page, and the Claim extractor stores a claim only when its quote appears word for word in that stored text.

### One search, end to end

The example is GS-02, "Flemish experts in telecom and 6G research in the New York and New Jersey area".

1. **Ask.** The page posts the question. The Store writes a Search row (who, when, depth) and the Search runner starts a DBOS workflow whose id is the search id.
2. **Chips (F1).** The Query parser, a Pydantic AI agent on Haiku 5.5, returns topics, institutions, place, ask and the text span of each. Code rejects a span that is not in the question. Institutions resolve against a local Research Organization Registry (ROR) table, places against GeoNames and the 2026 Core-Based Statistical Area (CBSA) list. An edited chip re-ranks results already fetched and reruns only the steps that read that chip.
3. **Network first (F3, Instant).** The Network ranker combines FTS5 bm25, sqlite-vec nearest neighbours over claim embeddings, and a location bucket with reciprocal rank fusion (RRF). No LLM sits in this path. "Inside the radius" and "travels or joins online" share one bucket, and location gets a lower weight than topic, so the 18 of 63 picks who were flown in are not pushed down. Each result shows the matched claim's quote as its reason. Seed-list people rank here too, marked New.
4. **Discovery (F4, F17).** The Query planner writes phrasings: the question as typed, institution-first, company-first for business asks, and Dutch rows. Source adapters run on per-source DBOS queues and return URL, rank, lane and the vendor snippet. For business asks the planner first lists Belgian and Flemish companies in the metro, then asks who leads each one, because company-first phrasing found 0 of 7 GS-03 picks until employers were named.
5. **Page read.** The Page reader fetches with httpx under a declared FN user agent, at one request a second per domain. trafilatura extracts text and htmldate the page date. Local Playwright handles pages that need JavaScript. Docling parses PDFs and keeps page numbers. A 403, a login wall or a CAPTCHA is a refusal, so FN does not retry the page and stores the vendor snippet as a Source of type "record" marked as vendor text. Each fetch becomes a Source row, and that row is the only cache.
6. **Claims (F5, F6).** The Claim extractor, Haiku 5.5 through Pydantic AI, returns claim drafts with the quote plus about 32 characters before and after it. A validator rejects any quote not found in the stored text and retries twice, after which the claim is stored as missing. A Haiku yes-or-no call per claim drops quotes that do not support their claim. The date falls back from htmldate to sitemap or RSS, then the first Wayback capture ("seen no later than"), then the read date. The extractor also emits availability claims from dated pages that show a US talk, visit or online appearance, and a location claim inside the metro sets "lives in the US".
7. **Records without a page.** OpenAlex, FRIS, Wikidata, EDGAR and iTunes answers are stored as a Source of type "record": the response text, the field used as the quote, and a human-readable URL such as openalex.org/A..., wikidata.org/wiki/Q... or the sec.gov filing. Imported rows become "imported sheet, row N, sheet date" and manual edits become "staff edit by user, date". Every shown claim then has a URL or a record reference, a quote and a date.
8. **Evidence per candidate (Extended, Deep).** For the top 20 candidates (top 100 at Deep) the Search runner fills each empty field in a fixed order and logs the cost of each step:
   1. Wikidata P69, P108, P27, P19, plus Kunstenpunt ids P12674 and P5068
   2. OpenAlex affiliation years
   3. FRIS persons and Biblio UGent
   4. EDGAR, for business people
   5. iTunes podcast-episode search by full name
   6. Staatsblad founding deeds of the candidate's Belgian employer, at Deep only: municipality of birth or residence, with register numbers, birth dates and street addresses removed
   7. Parallel Search name queries: name plus employer, `<naam> Vlaamse OR Belgische` on the press list, one Dutch role-change query such as `wordt CEO`

   A tie lookup stops only on a Flemish or Dutch claim. It continues past Belgian or none, so a Wikidata "citizen of Belgium" does not hide a KU Leuven degree further down. The free lookups (steps 1 to 3) also run at Quick, so Quick never shows a "not checked" label.
9. **Tie check.** The Tie typer alone sets the tie type. The extractor emits only the institution and the relation. Every Belgian ROR organization gets a region rule: Flemish Region is Flemish, Brussels follows a hand-set language column (VUB Flemish, ULB Belgian), Wallonia is Belgian, and the Netherlands is Dutch. This covers Vlerick, Karel de Grote, the Antwerp Royal Academy and the College of Europe, which a five-university list would miss. A company tie uses the postcode on the employer's own page. The label is flemish, belgian, dutch or none found, with the claim ids behind it. A name never sets a tie.
10. **Dedup (F9).** The Person resolver checks shared identifiers first: ORCID, OpenAlex id, Wikidata QID, FRIS uuid or canonical profile URL. A folded name key groups possible matches and RapidFuzz scores them. Dutch and English nickname tables raise a pair to a flag only when one more fact matches (employer, city or source). Borderline pairs go to one Sonnet 5.5 call that picks the matching listed person or none. Two borderline name-only matches inside one result show as one card marked "possibly two people". Nothing merges automatically.
11. **Rank and stream (F7).** The Candidate ranker combines lanes with RRF. At Extended and Deep, Sonnet 5.5 orders the top 50 with a reason that cites claim ids, and the RRF order stands if that call fails. The workflow body writes card patches to the DBOS stream, which DBOS documents as exactly-once, and a FastAPI server-sent events endpoint serves them. A person shows as a placeholder ("someone at Nokia Bell Labs") until a name and one checked claim exist.
12. **Reopen (F2).** A reopened search renders from stored search-result rows joined to current person and claim rows. The stream is used only while a run is in progress, so a person deleted, merged or skipped since shows correctly.
13. **Decide, store, export (F10 to F15).** Add is one SQLite transaction: person, claims shown and decision. Skip writes a decision the resolver reads next time. Merge re-points records and keeps both sets of sources. Keep separate stores a "not the same person" judgement. XlsxWriter writes XLSX with reason, quote, date and URL columns, and the `csv` module writes CSV. openpyxl reads the old sheet once, every cell as text, through the Person resolver.
14. **Cost, time and eval (NFR).** Every step writes a StepLog row with OpenTelemetry GenAI field names and its own `cost_usd`. genai-prices prices LLM calls and a price table copied from vendor pages prices the rest. Inspect AI runs this same workflow on the gold set. Quick on all 28 cases costs about $2.50 and runs on every prompt or model change.

### What each depth switches on

| Depth | Stages | Models | Cap (tune on the gold set) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Instant | Network ranker over the list and seed lists | None while ranking; Haiku parses chips in parallel | p50 under 2 s, p95 under 4 s |
| Quick | Planner, about 20 web calls across the lanes, page read of the top 30, claims, free tie lookups | Haiku | 2 min, $0.25 |
| Extended | Adds evidence per candidate for the top 20, ordering of the top 50, dedup on borderline pairs | Haiku; Sonnet to order and dedup | 15 min, $2 |
| Deep | Adds targets by institution, company, program and Flemish institution, one worker per target, co-authors and co-speakers of people found, rounds that fill gaps, evidence for the top 100 | Haiku; Sonnet to plan | 8 h, $25 |

Deep starts from the Extended shortlist and the staff's Add and Skip choices on it. It stops when the capture-recapture estimate of people not yet found stays flat for two rounds, or at the cap, and its last step lists what is still missing.

### The six lanes

A lane is one group of sources that produces candidates on its own.

| Precedent pages | The same API limited to delegation, mission, chamber and alumni domains | People picked before (De Cock 2022 and 2023, Vanholder 2022 and 2024) | 33 of Exa's 44 raw hits came from such pages |
| Academic graph | OpenAlex with a free key; two queries, Belgian affiliation and place only | Researchers with dated affiliations, with or without a tie | GS-02 3 of 3, GS-10 4 of 4 |
| Flemish research | FRIS SOAP services, projects by topic then researchers | Flanders-based experts who could travel | 11 of 13 Flanders-based picks by name; "6G" 58 projects, 69 people |
| Business filings | SEC EDGAR full-text search with edgartools | Officers of US public companies, Form D founders | De Cock, Burggraeve, Sweldens, Van Hentenryck; no academics |
| Seed lists | KVAB yearbook 2025, Francqui fellows, Belgian Climate Centre catalogue, BAEF alumni page and the US honorary consul page, scraped once (5,940 rows) | People with a known tie, at Instant speed | KVAB lists Maes, Trouet, Dewulf, Sweldens; Francqui 434 fellows incl. Goemans; Climate Centre 453 experts incl. Trouet, Bruyninckx |

## Benchmark plan

The benchmark decides one thing, the backend for the topic web search and precedent lanes, and whether FindAll should replace the FN Deep workers. Only that step changes between arms. The other lanes, models and caps stay fixed.

### Cases and scoring

- Run the 28 scorable cases at Quick and Extended. Report the 3 controls separately; they are not scorable.
- Run Deep with arm A on the hard-group cases: business GS-03, GS-08, GS-22, GS-33; creative GS-11, GS-12, GS-21, GS-30; Flanders-based GS-13, GS-16, GS-28; low footprint GS-24, GS-31. About 17 of the 33 people the sweep labelled Flanders-based are actually US partners, so relabel the group before scoring.
- Report every number per group, and with and without the 2022 economic mission cases (GS-01, GS-02, GS-03, GS-04, GS-10).
- Input is the question as typed plus one rewrite with synonyms. A phrasing that names the employer runs once as an upper bound and never counts.
- A pick counts as found only through a stored claim from a page dated before the event (htmldate or first Wayback capture) that does not mention the event. Raw recall is reported next to it.
- Names match picks one to one with the duplicate rule.
- A rate-limited or failed call is retried and logged as a failure, never read as zero results.
- Compare arms with paired per-case differences and a 95% bootstrap interval. When a difference falls inside the interval, decide on cost per held-out pick.

### Metrics

- Held-out and raw recall of known picks in the top 30, per depth
- Precision@5 by human review: an LLM judge grades topic, ability to attend and tie, then a delegation staff member reviews the pooled top 5 per case and arm (an estimated 300 to 400 people); the judge's grades count only once it agrees with the reviewer on a sample of 50
- Tie-label accuracy on found picks with a known tie
- Citation accuracy: share of shown claims whose quote appears word for word on the stored page and that the judge marks as supported (target 100%)
- Time per search (p50, p90) and cost per search and per pick found, per depth
- Two runs per case at Quick for the two leading arms, to measure run-to-run variance
- One run of arm A with extraction on Sonnet 5.5 as a quality baseline; Haiku stays if tie and citation accuracy stay inside the interval

### The result that decides

1. Parallel Search stays the backend unless B or D beats A on held-out recall at Extended outside the interval.
2. If A and B pooled beat A alone outside the interval, run both and combine with RRF.
3. FindAll replaces the FN Deep workers only if it finds more picks in the Flanders-based and creative groups, with precision@5 at least as good, at no more than twice the cost per pick found.
4. Task joins Extended only if A + Task beats A on held-out recall or tie accuracy outside the interval.
5. Each depth must raise held-out recall over the depth below it on the same cases. If one does not, fix that depth before adding any lane.
6. The chosen setup must reach 3 good picks with correct tie labels in the top 5 in at least half the cases. If no arm does, the next round adds the vendor research APIs listed under Not doing.

## Parts to borrow

### Search runner

- Planner to targets, one worker per target, then merge with identifier-first dedup ([A-MapReduce](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01331))
- A shared table of targets and a round that fills empty rows and unsettled ties ([Web2BigTable](https://github.com/web2bigtable/web2bigtable))
- Workers per depth (1, 2 to 3, 3 to 5, 5 to 10, at most 20) and a brief per worker with objective, output, sources, tools and scope ([Anthropic multi-agent post](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system))

### Eval runner

- The Scout setup, bootstrap 95% intervals, the provider adapters and the dry-run mode ([exa-labs/benchmarks](https://github.com/exa-labs/benchmarks))
- The held-out-record rule and the Q, C and E phrasings, with E as an upper bound (round-2 sweep)
- Recall pooled across options, a time cap per depth, cost per correct result, and constraints added one at a time: topic, place, tie, ask ([NewsCatcher](https://www.newscatcherapi.com/blog-posts/web-search-api-benchmarks-q3-2026))
- Precision and recall per case, with the rate of runs that stopped too early or padded the list ([DeepSearchQA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20975)), graded by one fixed model ([cookbook grader](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-cookbooks/tree/main/evals/agentic_search))
- F1 per column, row and cell ([DeepWideSearch](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20168)), and the average over several runs plus a classification of misses ([WideSearch](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07999))
- Each ask split into criteria, each graded 0, 0.5 or 1 against a live check ([PeopleSearchBench](https://github.com/LessieAI/people-search-bench))
- A judge that reads each returned page against the query, and a test that hides people from the imported list and checks they are found again ([Exa people-search benchmark](https://exa.ai/blog/people-search-benchmark))
- Citation accuracy and the count of supported citations ([DeepResearch Bench FACT](https://github.com/Ayanami0730/deep_research_bench)): the URL resolves, the quote is verbatim, the judge confirms it ([Cited but Not Verified](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06635))
- One-to-one name matching with `linear_sum_assignment` ([autoevals ListContains](https://github.com/braintrustdata/autoevals))
- Repeat runs to check that names stay the same, at low temperature ([LLMScholarBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08873)), and a rewrite of each ask with synonyms ([benchmark bias study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05018))
- A judge that may answer "unknown", human checks on a sample until judge and human agree, and pass^k over repeated runs ([Anthropic evals post](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents))

## Risks and terms to check

The largest risk is low recall in the business, creative and low-footprint groups.

### Facts that did not hold

- Raw recall overstates discovery. 33 of Exa's 44 Q results sat only on the same event's record. With those records and LinkedIn and Instagram removed, Q found 6 of 74 picks with Exa and 5 of 74 with the Parallel MCP. Five picks were never found by any phrasing in either backend (GS-16 and GS-21 people).
- The Parallel MCP figures describe the MCP's default mode only. They say nothing about the Search API in fast mode with domain lists.
- Nobody has measured whether Dutch query rows add recall over English, because the probe ran no English control.
- nokia.com and kuleuven.be robots.txt returned 403 to plain clients. The Vetter quote from Nokia could not be reproduced, so it shows the method works, not a gold-set hit.
- The Dutch press quotes for Boutelegier, D'Hulst and Herdewyn exist only as vendor excerpts, because the publishers returned 403. Under the new rule they can be stored as vendor-text records.
- Parallel basis and FindAll basis carry no dates, basis excerpts can be null, and nothing states that Parallel excerpts are verbatim.
- Exa's people index is described as "1B+ LinkedIn profiles", which reverses round 1's plan to use `category: people`.
- ROR puts VUB in Brussels Capital, and the ORCID lookup added no ties.
- FRIS keyword search fails with a SOAP fault, and its person records are fragmented (35 stub records for Hans Bruyninckx).
- FastAPI does not replay events (reopen renders from stored rows instead), FTS5 trigrams do not catch misspellings, Inspect logs record no dollars, and openpyxl has had no release since 2024-06-28.
- Assigning app access by group in Entra needs P1 or P2, and delegation staff may sit in a tenant other than FIT's.

### Terms

Terms do not gate the personal prototype (see Decisions). The clauses that would matter for a product are in Appendix B and `findings.json`: Parallel Customer Terms 2(c)(vi)(B), 2(c)(xi) and 4(b), Exa 4.2(a), the Perplexity Search Addendum 2.3, the Flemish press reservations and GDPR. Attribution is still owed to GeoNames (CC BY 4.0), Onomaverse (CC BY 4.0) OpenStreetMap on the map (ODbL), and Wiktionary (CC BY-SA) if used.

### Quotas and limits

- **OpenAlex.** A free key gives $1 a day (about 10,000 filter calls or 1,000 searches) and 30 requests a second. Without a key you get $0.10 a day shared per IP and no cursor paging; the round-1 probe hit HTTP 429 with a wait of about 7,800s. A team member has to confirm the account email.
- **Exa.** The keyless MCP throttles at about 6 parallel calls, and 6 calls were rate-limited in the sweep. The API includes $10 of free credit a month.
- **Parallel.** The pricing page lists no free tier (a Parallel blog post mentions a $5 monthly credit with a card). Search allows 600 requests a minute and Task 2,000.
- **iTunes.** About 20 calls a minute.
- **Wikidata Query Service.** 60s timeout and a User-Agent header required. The WMDE-hosted MCP gave no HTTP response on 2026-10-28.
- **SEC EDGAR.** 10 requests a second, with a contact address in the User-Agent.
- **Nominatim.** One request at a time, a custom User-Agent and a permanent cache. FN calls it only at import and edit.
- **Excel.** 65,530 hyperlinks per sheet.

### Technical risks

- Nobody has measured whether Instant meets p50 under 2s. The Qwen3 query embedding and the Haiku parse are the unknowns.
- DBOS on SQLite polls for changes, and its stream writes can repeat on retry. DBOS also releases often (3.4.0 and 3.5.0a0 came out on the same day), so pin the version.
- sqlite-vec is still before 1.0.
- To allow two tenants, oauth2-proxy needs `insecure_oidc_skip_issuer_verification=true`, which leaves the tenant check to the allowed-tenant list.
- FindAll and Entity Search are public betas, with 30 days' notice of breaking changes.
- Precedent pages can postdate the event they stand in for. The benchmark should report how many found picks rest only on later pages.

## Not doing

We leave out anything that fails a knockout, duplicates a pick, or serves a need the MVP doc does not state.

- **LinkedIn data outside the benchmark arms.** Exa people (B-people) and Apify (arm E) run in the benchmark; Clado, Pearch, Lessie, Sixtyfour, People Data Labs, Coresignal, Bright Data and Apollo were re-rated and dropped as duplicates of those two arms or for lack of a topic search. FN itself never logs in to or scrapes LinkedIn.
- **Claims from vendor answers.** Basis reasoning and generated answers never become claims. A vendor snippet is stored only for a page FN cannot fetch.
- **Organizations as records**, which are out of the MVP. Companies appear only as temporary planner targets.
- **Institutions invented by the model.** Institutions resolve against ROR or stay as quoted text, which fixes the POC's "A Major Flemish University" rows.
- **Contact details**, which are out of the MVP. The email field in the europarl record is ignored.
- **Scheduled discovery or verification.** The role-change check runs only on demand inside a search.
- **Vendor research APIs in round one**: You.com Research and Search, Perplexity Agent API, Valyu DeepResearch, Exa Agent, Exa Websets and Parallel Entity Search. Diffbot Knowledge Graph was dropped in the re-rating. They join only if no round-one arm reaches the precision target, and each has its own obstacle:
  - Exa Websets: needs a paid plan.
  - Entity Search: returns no citations and scored 8% at three constraints.
- **Claude's web_search and web_fetch tools as the backend.** Our own fetcher handles fetching, Claude search costs $10 per 1,000 against $1 for Parallel fast mode, and web_fetch returns no page date.
- **The Message Batches API for overnight runs.** Synchronous calls inside DBOS steps keep one code path.
- **EU endpoint, zero data retention and the Exa enterprise MSA.** Each needs an enterprise agreement, which K5 rules out.
- **Firecrawl, hosted or self-hosted.** Local Playwright covers JavaScript pages at no per-page cost, FN does not route refusals around bot protection, and the self-hosted server is AGPL.
- **Other podcast and video APIs**: YouTube Data API and captions, Podcast Index, Listen Notes, Taddy, Podscan, Podchaser, Spotify, TED and reading whole RSS feeds. The re-rating kept all of them dropped: none beats the iTunes call on a gold case, and YouTube captions cannot read third-party talks.
- **Small or name-only directories**: OceanExpert, NYC payroll, the europarl MEP API, the Kunstenpunt databank (it closes in 2027 and its data is already in Wikidata), Expertendatabank, VAF, MoMu, Flanders Literature, Getty ULAN, VAi Archiefhub, AP Arts, LVMH Prize, TheFashionDB, UiTdatabank, Onroerend Erfgoed, VIW, VRT MAX, NAF, De Ondernemer, the Georgia fact sheet and the BelCham readiness page. Each fails K1 or covers 2 picks or fewer.
- **Other people databases**: People Data Labs, Apollo, Clearbit, Crunchbase, PitchBook, the Dealroom API, Clado, Pearch, the Juicebox product and hireEZ. They lack sourced claims, sell contact data or duplicate the LinkedIn benchmark arms.
- **Other search APIs**: Brave, Tavily, Google Custom Search, Bing, Gemini grounding and Linkup. They duplicate Parallel and Exa, and Gemini grounding returns no quote or date.
- **Other academic sources**: Semantic Scholar, the ORCID API, Crossref, dblp, CORDIS, the Lirias harvest, OpenAIRE, PatentsView and a separate NIH RePORTER client. OpenAlex covers them.
- **Other job runners and backends**: Temporal, Inngest, Trigger.dev, Hatchet, Restate, Celery, PocketBase, Supabase and ElectricSQL. DBOS does the same job without a server.
- **Other tooling**: Langfuse, BAML, Splink, dedupe, Zingg, ParadeDB (AGPL), Typesense, Meilisearch, and the Cohere and Jina rerankers. Each duplicates a pick.
- **Round 1's TypeScript adopts** (Workflow SDK, Better Auth, SheetJS, promptfoo, normalize-url, AI SDK streaming). They remain runner-ups only, because the stack is Python.
- **A separate "tie basis" column and a "Dutch with Belgian operations" tie type.** Neither is in the MVP data model.
- **A cross-encoder in the Instant path and voyage embeddings**, until the gold run shows they gain enough to justify them.
- **A separate verification queue.** The sourced claim is the check, as the POC lessons say.
- **Parallel Task API in the prototype.** It is the largest Extended cost and returns undated URLs FN fetches anyway; it runs as the A + Task benchmark arm instead.
- **Phoenix tracing.** StepLog plus the Inspect log viewer cover development, and Phoenix would be a third store of provider output.
- **Replaying the DBOS stream to reopen a search.** Streams are append-only and retention deletes history; reopen renders from stored rows.
